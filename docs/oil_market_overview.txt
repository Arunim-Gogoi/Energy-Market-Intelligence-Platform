Global Oil Market Overview

Oil markets are shaped by the balance between OPEC+ production quotas and non-OPEC supply
growth, particularly from the United States shale sector. Demand growth in recent years has
been driven primarily by Asia, with China and India accounting for a large share of
incremental consumption, while demand in mature OECD economies has been roughly flat or
declining due to efficiency gains and the early stages of electrification in transport.

Upstream investment cycles tend to lag price signals by one to two years: a period of high
prices typically leads to increased capital expenditure on exploration and development,
which then adds supply a few years later, often coinciding with softer prices. Spare
production capacity, concentrated mainly in Saudi Arabia and a handful of Gulf producers,
acts as a key buffer against supply shocks from geopolitical events, sanctions, or
unplanned outages.

Refining margins and regional price differentials (such as Brent versus WTI) are influenced
by logistics bottlenecks, refinery maintenance schedules, and shifts in crude quality
demand as refiners adjust to changing product specifications, including lower-sulfur fuel
standards.
