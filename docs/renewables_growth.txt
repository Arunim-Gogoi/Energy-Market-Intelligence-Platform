Renewable Capacity Growth Trends

Solar photovoltaic and onshore wind have become the cheapest source of new electricity
generation in most markets, driven by steep cost declines in panel manufacturing, larger
turbine designs, and improved capacity factors. China remains the largest single market for
new renewable capacity additions, followed by the United States, India, and the European
Union, with policy support mechanisms such as auctions, tax credits, and renewable
purchase obligations playing a major role in project bankability.

Grid integration challenges are a growing bottleneck: intermittency from solar and wind
requires expanded transmission capacity, flexible generation or storage to balance supply,
and in some markets curtailment of renewable output during periods of oversupply. Battery
storage costs have fallen sharply, making short-duration storage increasingly viable
alongside renewable projects, though long-duration storage solutions remain more costly
and less mature.

Offshore wind is expanding beyond its traditional strongholds in Northern Europe into new
markets in Asia and the United States, though project timelines are sensitive to supply
chain constraints for specialized vessels, turbine components, and port infrastructure.
